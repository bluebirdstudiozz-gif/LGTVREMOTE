We built LG TV Remote to turn your phone into a remote control for smart TVs and streaming devices on your local network, and to let you cast photos, videos, and IPTV channels to those devices. This policy describes what data the App accesses, what stays on your device, what is shared with diagnostic and (currently inactive) advertising partners, and the choices you have.

Please read this policy carefully. By installing or using the App, you agree to the practices described here. If you do not agree, please do not use the App.

1. App overview & scope

LG TV Remote discovers and controls televisions and streaming devices — including LG (webOS), Samsung (Tizen), Roku, Apple TV, Android TV/Google TV, Vizio, Philips, and generic DLNA/UPnP devices — over your local Wi-Fi network. All remote-control communication (button presses, pairing PINs, volume commands, app-launch requests, air-mouse pointer movement) happens directly between your phone and your TV on your own network. We do not operate a server that receives, stores, or relays your TV control commands. The App can also send a Wake-on-LAN signal to a saved TV's stored MAC address to power it on remotely over your local network.

The App also lets you cast photos, videos, and IPTV channels from your phone to supported TVs — via Google Cast (Chromecast/Google TV), DLNA, AirPlay, or a temporary local streaming link the App creates on your own Wi-Fi network — and offers a screen mirroring shortcut into your phone's own OS mirroring feature. Section 3 explains exactly how these work and what stays on your device. Section 4 explains our optional in-app support chat, the one feature in the App that sends data to a database we can access.

The App includes the Google AdMob and Unity Ads SDKs for showing advertisements, and Firebase Crashlytics, Google Analytics for Firebase, and Firebase Remote Config for diagnostics and update management. As of this version, ads are switched off app-wide, and — because of how the ad and analytics SDKs are linked together — this currently also means the App's analytics collection does not run either. Sections 6 and 7 explain exactly what that means and what each SDK is built to collect if/when re-enabled.

2. Data stored locally on your device

To remember your TVs, casting targets, and playlists between sessions, the App maintains a local database and local preference store (Android's Room and DataStore libraries) stored entirely within the App's private storage on your device. This data is never transmitted to us, and is never sent to Firebase or our advertising partners either — we have no backend server for it and no access to it. It is also explicitly excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, including the pairing tokens and TLS certificate pins described below. It includes:

One exception, and only if you start a support chat. If you choose to message us in the App (Section 4), a short summary of which TV brands and platforms you have saved — for example "LG (webOS), Samsung" — and how many devices that is, is attached to your message so we can help without asking you for it. The details that identify your home are not included: no custom names or room labels you typed, no IP or MAC addresses, no pairing credentials, no playlists, and no cast library entries. If you never open the support chat, nothing in this section leaves your device.
  • Device identifiers: local IP addresses, MAC addresses (used only to send a Wake-on-LAN signal), device model, and any custom name you assign to a saved TV. These labels are stored only so you can recognise your own devices in the App and stay on your device.
  • Pairing credentials: pairing tokens, client certificates, TLS certificate pins, and session keys issued by your TV during pairing (e.g. LG webOS, Android TV, Samsung, Vizio, Philips), stored so you don't have to re-pair every time.
  • IPTV playlists you add: the name you give a playlist, its source (a direct M3U/M3U8 URL, or an Xtream Codes login resolved to a playlist URL), and the resulting list of channel names, logos, and stream links. See Section 3 for an important note about Xtream Codes credentials.
  • Cast library: if you cast a photo or video from your phone, the App remembers a reference to it (the file's content link, display name, and file type) so it reappears in your cast library next time — the file itself is never copied or duplicated by the App.
  • App preferences: your selected language, theme/appearance, and onboarding/tutorial completion status.

All of the above is removed automatically if you uninstall the App, or manually if you remove individual saved devices, playlists, or cast library items from within the App, or clear the App's storage via Android's system Settings.

3. Casting, screen mirroring & IPTV playlists

Casting photos and videos

When you choose to cast a photo or video, the App uses Android's system photo/video picker to let you select it — the App is never granted broad access to your photo library or storage, only to the specific item(s) you pick. To deliver that file to a TV that can't read it directly from your phone (Chromecast/Google TV, DLNA, and some AirPlay scenarios), the App briefly runs a small local web server bound to your phone's own Wi-Fi address, so the TV can fetch the file over your local network. This server is only reachable on your local Wi-Fi network and is not exposed to the internet.

Casting to Chromecast/Google TV, DLNA, and AirPlay devices

The App uses the Google Cast SDK (part of Google Play services) to discover Chromecast/Google TV-enabled devices on your network and to start, control, and end casting sessions. It also discovers and casts to DLNA-compatible devices and AirPlay (Apple TV) receivers using the same local-network discovery and communication described in Section 1 — no separate account or server is involved. Discovering cast targets and controlling playback happens directly between your phone and the receiving device on your local network.

Screen mirroring

Screen mirroring hands off to your phone's own OS-level mirroring/casting surface (or, on devices without one, a generic system settings screen) to send your display to a supported TV. This does not route through us or through Firebase.

IPTV playlists

The App lets you add your own IPTV source — either a direct M3U/M3U8 playlist URL, or an Xtream Codes login (server address, username, and password), which the App resolves into a playlist URL for you. No channel content ships with the App; every playlist is one you supply.

Important: for Xtream Codes-style logins, the resolved playlist URL contains your username and password as part of the URL itself. This URL is stored only in the App's private local storage on your device (Section 2), and is used only to fetch your channel list and stream video directly from your IPTV provider — it is never sent to us, to Firebase, or to any advertising partner. If you remove the playlist from the App, this stored URL and its credentials are deleted with it. Because this URL contains a password, treat sharing your device backup or screenshots of the App's playlist screen with the same care you would treat sharing that password directly.

When you play or cast a channel, the App may log which streaming format was used (for example "HLS" or "DASH") for diagnostic purposes — never the playlist URL, login credentials, or channel names themselves. See Section 7 for the current status of this logging.

Voice input

The App's microphone button hands off to your device's built-in speech-recognition system via Android's standard RecognizerIntent. Audio is captured and processed by your phone's own default assistant/speech-recognition app — not by this App — and is not sent to us.

4. In-app support chat

The App includes an in-app chat so you can report a problem or ask a question without leaving the App, creating an account, or sending an email. This is the one feature in the App that sends data to a database we can read — every other section of this policy describes data that stays on your device or goes only to Google. When you send a message in the chat:

  • The text you type is sent to and stored in Cloud Firestore, a Google Firebase database, under a conversation identified only by an anonymous, device-scoped identity created automatically via Firebase Authentication (Anonymous) — you are never asked to sign in or provide your name or email address to use it. Reinstalling the App creates a new anonymous identity and a fresh, unlinked conversation.
  • A short technical snapshot is attached to your message, so a report like "my TV won't connect" arrives with the context needed to answer it: your phone's manufacturer/model and Android version, the App's version, which TV brands and platforms you have saved and how many (never their names, IP, or MAC addresses), what kind of network your phone is on right now and whether something is blocking discovery (airplane mode, Wi-Fi off, a VPN — never a Wi-Fi name, password, or IP address), your device's language/region setting, how many days ago the App was installed, and your app's Firebase Analytics instance ID (used only to look up your own device's diagnostic events for this conversation, per Section 7 — not your name, email, or phone number). Pairing credentials, PINs, IPTV playlists/logins, and cast library entries are never included.
  • While you have the chat screen open, our support staff can request a refreshed copy of that same snapshot so they can see the current state of a problem rather than the state when you last typed. It refreshes only the fields listed above, and never your message text or anything not already listed here.
  • While handling your conversation, our support staff may add internal notes and category tags to it (for example "pairing" or "bug"). These are our own working notes, are stored separately so the App can never read them back to you, and are deleted together with the conversation (Section 11).
  • Messages are capped in length, and the App and our backend both rate-limit how quickly messages can be sent, to keep the chat usable and deter automated abuse. If a conversation is used abusively, we may block it — a blocked conversation's messages stay only on your device and are never delivered to us, so the App's own behavior looks unchanged to you.
  • Your messages are visible to our support staff through an access-restricted internal tool. They are not publicly accessible, are not used for advertising, and are not shared with our advertising partners (Section 6).
  • Once a conversation is closed by our support staff, it and its full message history are scheduled for automatic deletion after 7 days. If you (or we) send another message before that window elapses, the scheduled deletion is cancelled and the conversation continues normally. A conversation we haven't closed persists until we do. In cases where sensitive content is pasted into a chat by mistake (for example a phone number or an unrelated account detail), we can also delete a specific message or the whole conversation immediately, ahead of that schedule.
  • If you'd rather not use the chat, the "Prefer email?" option inside it opens your device's email app addressed to our support inbox instead — nothing is sent until you choose to send that email yourself.

6. Advertising & the Advertising ID

Current status: ads are disabled. As of this version, the App does not initialize the Google Mobile Ads SDK, does not show any ads, and does not offer any in-app purchase — the App is currently free with no monetization active. The rest of this section is a forward-looking disclosure of what the App is built to do if ads are re-enabled in a future release.

If enabled, the App would be supported by ads delivered through Google AdMob, with Unity Ads as a mediated demand partner within the same ad units. Depending on your settings and region, showing ads would involve the following being shared with Google and Unity:

  • Your device's Advertising ID (a resettable, non-permanent identifier — not your name, email, or phone number).
  • Coarse device and app information (device model, OS version, app version, language, general/coarse location derived from IP address) used for ad targeting and fraud prevention.
  • Ad interaction data (impressions, clicks) used for measurement.

The App is built to show three ad formats — banner, interstitial (full-screen), and app-open ads — with an interstitial cadence designed to wait for a meaningful amount of active use and a short pause since your last button press before ever showing one, rather than interrupting you mid-action. None of this currently runs.

Even while ads are disabled, the App still runs Google's consent-gathering flow (the User Messaging Platform, or UMP) on first launch in regions that require it — see Section 9 — so the consent infrastructure is already in place for if/when ads are turned back on, without exposing you to any ads in the meantime. You can reset or opt out of ad personalization generally at any time in your device's Android system settings under Settings → Privacy → Ads.

7. Crash reporting & analytics (Firebase)

LG TV Remote includes Firebase Crashlytics, Google Analytics for Firebase, and Firebase Remote Config (Google-owned services) to help us find and fix bugs, understand app usage, and manage app updates. This is separate from the advertising SDKs described in Section 6 and from the support chat described in Section 4.

Analytics is currently inactive too, tied to the same switch as ads. The App's build intentionally delays Google Analytics measurement until the Google Mobile Ads SDK initializes — and because ads are switched off (Section 6), that initialization never runs. In practice this means the custom analytics events described below are not currently being collected; every logged event is silently dropped on-device. Crashlytics is unaffected by this and remains active, since it initializes independently at app startup.

What Crashlytics collects (active)

When the App crashes or encounters a handled (non-fatal) error, Crashlytics sends Google a crash report containing the stack trace, Android OS version, device manufacturer/model, and app version, plus a small set of diagnostic tags we attach ourselves: the connected TV brand and protocol (e.g. "Samsung", "SAMSUNG_WS"), the network port used, the current connection phase (e.g. "connecting", "pairing", "sending"), an error-context label, and short breadcrumb log lines describing what the app was doing (e.g. "Sending KEY_VOLUP"). Our code is deliberately written to never include pairing tokens/auth keys, PINs, MAC addresses, full IP addresses, certificate fingerprints, or IPTV playlist URLs/credentials in this diagnostic data.

If you give a saved TV a custom name, that name is never uploaded to a crash report — not even in part. A TV name is user-authored free text that routinely carries a person's or household's name, or a room in their home (for example "Sarah's Bedroom TV"), which is exactly the kind of identifying detail this diagnostic pipeline is built to exclude. At most, a non-identifying "shape" of the name (whether it was left blank, and roughly how long or how many non-standard characters it had) may be recorded, to help distinguish "no name" from "an unusually long name caused this crash" — never the name's content.

What Analytics is built to collect (currently inactive — see above)

If and when Analytics measurement is enabled, Google Analytics for Firebase would collect standard usage signals automatically (app opens, sessions, engagement, device model, OS version, app version, language, and country/region derived from IP address), plus our own custom events covering: discovery and connection outcomes (by TV brand/protocol, never IP or MAC address, pairing tokens, or PINs), pairing success/failure, remote-control command counts (bucketed into ranges, not exact values), casting/DLNA/AirPlay discovery and session outcomes, IPTV playlist and playback outcomes (never the playlist URL, login credentials, or channel names), and app-experience events like update prompts and notification permission responses. This data is designed to be used in aggregate, to understand overall app health and usage trends — not to build individual user profiles or target advertising.

The one exception is a support conversation you started. If analytics measurement is active and you message us in the App, your app's Firebase Analytics instance ID is attached to that conversation (Section 4) so we can look up the events above for your device to diagnose the specific problem you reported. We would do this only for conversations you chose to start, only to answer them, and the link is deleted with the conversation.

What Remote Config and In-App Updates collect (active)

On each app launch, the App fetches a small set of configuration values from Firebase Remote Config — release-management flags such as the minimum supported app version and the latest available version — and may prompt or guide you through installing an update via the Google Play Store. These are non-personal feature flags and standard Play-services update mechanics, and do not include any information about your saved TVs, playlists, cast library, or pairing credentials.

9. Your consent choices (GDPR / UK / US)

If you are located in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the App presents a consent form (via Google's UMP SDK) the first time you open it, letting you choose whether to allow personalized advertising, non-personalized advertising only, or to review partner-by-partner choices. This consent flow runs regardless of whether ads are currently active (Section 6), so it's already in place if ads are turned back on.

Where a consent form applies to your region, you can revisit and change your choice at any time from within the App: Settings → Privacy Options. The App's Privacy Policy link is always available in Settings regardless of your region.

In-app "Report a Bug" / Contact Support

The App's Settings screen includes a "Report a Bug"/support option. This opens the in-app support chat described in Section 4 — see that section for exactly what's collected and how long it's kept. 10. No accounts, no app-specific server

LG TV Remote does not require you to create an account, sign in, or provide your name, email address, or phone number to use any feature of the App — including casting, IPTV, and the support chat. There is no cloud backend operated by us for the App's core TV-control functionality — the App does not sync your saved TVs, playlists, cast library, or settings across devices, and we have no server-side database of that data. Crash reports and (when active) usage analytics described in Section 7 are sent directly to Google's Firebase infrastructure, not to any server we run ourselves. IPTV streams and casting traffic go directly between your phone, your IPTV provider (for streams you add), and your TV or casting target — never through us.

The one exception is the in-app support chat (Section 4): messages you choose to send there are stored in Cloud Firestore, a Firebase database our support staff can read and reply to. You are never required to use this feature, and it never collects your name, email, or an account of any kind.

11. Data retention & deletion

Because all app-specific data (saved devices, pairing credentials, IPTV playlists, cast library references, and preferences) lives only in local storage on your device, it is retained for as long as the App remains installed and is permanently deleted when you uninstall the App or clear its storage. We have no ability to retain or recover this data after that point, since we never received a copy of it.

Support chat messages (Section 4) are retained until we close the conversation, after which they are scheduled for automatic deletion 7 days later unless a new message arrives in the meantime, in which case the scheduled deletion is cancelled and the conversation continues. Deleting a conversation deletes everything attached to it: the messages, the technical snapshot, the analytics link (Section 7), and our internal notes and tags. If you'd like a chat conversation deleted sooner, contact us using the details in Section 17 and we can remove it immediately.

Data collected by Firebase Crashlytics (Section 7), and by our advertising partners and Analytics if/when active (Sections 6–7), is retained and deleted according to each provider's own retention policies, linked in Section 8.

12. Children's privacy

LG TV Remote is a general-audience utility app, not directed at children under the age of 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your jurisdiction), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with information, please contact us using the details in Section 17 so we can address it.

13. Security

Communication between the App and your TVs happens over your local Wi-Fi network. Some TV brands' native control protocols (e.g. LG webOS, Roku, Apple TV, and older Samsung models) only support unencrypted local connections by design; this traffic never leaves your local network and is never routed through the internet or through us. The same local-network scope applies to casting and screen mirroring (Section 3): the App's local media server is only reachable from devices on your own Wi-Fi network.

Pairing credentials, TLS certificate pins, and IPTV login details stored locally (Sections 2 and 3) are kept within the App's private, sandboxed storage, which is not accessible to other apps on your device under normal Android security policy, and is excluded from cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Crash reporting (Section 7) is deliberately designed to exclude these credentials, along with PINs, MAC addresses, and full IP addresses, from anything sent to Firebase. Support chat messages (Section 4) are access-restricted to authorized BlueSpaceStudioz accounts.

14. Your rights (GDPR, CCPA & other state laws)

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to:

  • Access the categories of data collected about you by our diagnostic (and, if active, advertising) partners, or a support chat conversation you sent us — including the technical snapshot attached to it and any internal notes we added (Section 4).
  • Delete locally stored app data (including saved devices, playlists, and cast library entries) at any time by removing them in-app, or by uninstalling the App or clearing its storage; and request deletion of a support chat conversation as described in Section 11.
  • Opt out of personalized advertising and, where applicable under U.S. state law, opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your information with advertising partners, via the in-app Privacy Options (Section 9) and your device's ad settings — relevant only once/if ads are re-enabled (Section 6).
  • Object to or restrict processing carried out by our third-party crash-reporting, analytics, and (if active) advertising partners, subject to their own policies linked in Section 8.

Because we do not operate our own servers or hold a database of personal information about you (other than a support chat conversation you chose to start, Section 4), most data-subject requests regarding Firebase- or ad-related data should be directed to Google and Unity directly using the links in Section 8, or to us at the contact below and we will assist where we are able to.

15. International data transfers

Our diagnostic and (if active) advertising partners (Google/Firebase, Unity) may process data on servers located outside your country of residence, including in the United States, under the safeguards described in their respective privacy policies. This includes the Firebase infrastructure behind the in-app support chat (Section 4). Data related to TV control, casting, screen mirroring, and IPTV stays on your local network and is not transferred internationally by us.
