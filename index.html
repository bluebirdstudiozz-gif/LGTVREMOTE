1.What This App Does

LG TV Remote turns your phone into a remote control for smart TVs and media receivers on your local Wi-Fi network. It discovers TVs and Cast/DLNA receivers over your local network (LG webOS, Samsung Tizen, Roku, Apple TV, Android TV/Google TV, Vizio, Philips, and generic DLNA/UPnP devices), connects to them directly over your LAN to send remote-control commands (power, volume, navigation, app launch, air-mouse pointer control), lets you cast locally-picked photos/videos or IPTV playlists to a receiver, and includes an in-app support chat, ad-supported free access, and Google Play in-app update checks.

The App does not require you to create an account, and does not ask for your name, email address, or phone number to use its core remote-control features.

2.Information Collected Automatically

2.1 Analytics (Firebase Analytics)

The App uses Firebase Analytics to understand feature usage and diagnose connectivity problems (e.g., how often device discovery succeeds, how often a TV connection fails and why, IPTV playback errors, casting activity, air-mouse performance). These events are deliberately designed to exclude personal or network-identifying information — our own engineering documentation for this logging explicitly states that auth tokens, PINs, IP addresses, MAC addresses, and device names are never included. Events instead carry non-identifying data such as: TV brand/protocol category, connection outcome, error classification, network transport type (Wi-Fi/cellular/VPN/none), app version, and a Firebase-generated analytics installation identifier.

Firebase Analytics may also automatically collect standard device/diagnostic identifiers (such as an advertising identifier, general device model, OS version, language, and approximate location derived from IP address) as part of its standard SDK behavior.

2.2 Crash & Diagnostic Reporting (Firebase Crashlytics)

The App uses Firebase Crashlytics to automatically collect crash reports and diagnostic logs (device model, OS version, app version, stack traces) when the App crashes or behaves unexpectedly, to help us fix bugs.

2.3 Remote Configuration (Firebase Remote Config)

The App uses Firebase Remote Config to fetch non-personal configuration values (for example, minimum supported app version, to show update prompts). This does not involve personal data.

2.4 Advertising Identifiers

The App includes the Google AdMob and Unity Ads SDKs, along with the Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) consent SDK, for showing ads (see Section 6). Ads are currently switched off app-wide — the App does not initialize these ad SDKs and they do not run or collect any data in the current version. This section and Section 6 describe what would happen, and the data these SDKs are capable of collecting (advertising identifier, device/app information, general IP-based location), if and when ads are turned back on in a future version, without requiring you to install an update to see this disclosure take effect.

3.Local Network & On-Device Processing

Most of what this App does happens entirely on your device or directly between your device and your TV, over your own local Wi-Fi network, and never reaches any server we control:

  • Device discovery uses standard local-network discovery protocols — SSDP, mDNS/NSD, and DLNA/UPnP — broadcast on your local Wi-Fi network only. This traffic never leaves your LAN and is never sent to us.
  • TV control (remote-control button presses, air-mouse pointer movement, app launching, input switching) connects directly from your phone to your TV's IP address over your local network (e.g., WebSocket to LG webOS, HTTP to Roku/Apple TV, DLNA/UPnP commands). We do not operate, and this traffic never passes through, any server of ours.
  • Saved devices (TV name, IP/MAC address, pairing/auth tokens, TLS certificate pins) are stored only in a local database on your device. This data is explicitly excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, and is deleted when you uninstall the App or remove the device in-app. It is never uploaded to us.
  • Casting and local media streaming: when you cast a locally-picked photo, video, or IPTV stream to a TV/Cast receiver, the App runs a small local HTTP server on your phone (via NanoHTTPD) solely so your TV can fetch that file over your LAN. This server is only reachable on your local network and is not accessible over the internet.
  • IPTV playlists: if you add an IPTV playlist (M3U URL or Xtream login), your device connects directly to the URL/provider you supply to fetch the playlist and streams; we do not proxy, store, or see this content or your provider credentials.
  • Voice input: the microphone button hands off to your device's built-in speech-recognition system (via Android's RecognizerIntent). Audio is captured and processed by your phone's default assistant/speech-recognition app, not by this App, and is not sent to us.

5.Support Chat

The App includes an in-app support chat (Firebase Authentication + Cloud Firestore). When you open or use support chat:

  • An anonymous Firebase Authentication identity is created for your install (no name, email, or password required or collected for this).
  • Your messages are stored in a Firestore document tied to that anonymous ID, so our support team can read and reply to your thread.
  • To help us answer your question without back-and-forth, the App automatically attaches basic technical context to your thread: device manufacturer/model, Android version, app version, a summary of TV brand types you have saved (never IP/MAC addresses or device names), current network type and any detected connectivity issue, your device's language setting, how many days since install, and the Firebase Analytics instance ID (used only to correlate your ticket with the anonymized usage funnel described in Section 2.1, for troubleshooting).
  • If you instead choose to email us, your message and email address are sent directly via your phone's own email app to our support address below and are not otherwise stored by us.

6.Advertising

Current status: ads are disabled. As of this version, the App does not show any ads and does not initialize any advertising SDK. The App is currently free with no ads and no in-app purchases. The rest of this section is a forward-looking disclosure of what the App is built to do if ads are re-enabled in a future release — so you're informed in advance rather than only after an update changes behavior.

If enabled, the App would be supported by ads shown via Google AdMob, with Unity Ads as a mediation partner, using the Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) to manage consent (including GDPR/EEA consent prompts and, where applicable, an "AdChoices" opt-out). These partners may collect your advertising identifier and other device data to select, serve, and measure ads, and may set/read identifiers for that purpose.

Your choices:

  • On first launch in the EEA/UK (and elsewhere where required), a consent prompt lets you manage ad personalization choices via the UMP dialog.
  • You can reset or limit your advertising identifier, or opt out of personalized/interest-based ads generally, in your device's Settings (Google Settings → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads on newer Android versions).
  • Google AdMob: policies.google.com/technologies/ads
  • Unity Ads: unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

The App does not currently offer in-app purchases or subscriptions, and (per the status note above) does not currently show ads either — it is currently free with no monetization active.

11.Data Retention

  • Locally stored data (saved TVs, IPs, MAC addresses, pairing/auth tokens, app preferences) remains on your device only and is deleted when you uninstall the App or remove it in-app.
  • Support chat data is retained in Firestore for as long as needed to resolve your inquiry and for a reasonable period afterward for quality/reference purposes; reinstalling the App creates a new anonymous identity and does not carry your prior thread forward.
  • Analytics and crash data are retained according to Firebase's default retention settings (Google Analytics for Firebase data is generally retained for up to 14 months unless configured otherwise).

12.Data Security

We rely on the security controls provided by Google Firebase and Google/Unity's advertising infrastructure for any data that reaches our backend (support chat). Communication between the App and your TV happens over your local network; some TV brands' native control protocols (LG webOS, Roku, Apple TV, older Samsung models) only support unencrypted local connections, which is why the App permits cleartext traffic strictly for on-device-to-TV communication on your own LAN — this traffic never leaves your local network. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure, but we take reasonable measures to protect information consistent with industry practice.

13.International Data Transfers

Firebase (Google), AdMob, and Unity Ads operate data centers globally, which means information they process (as described above) may be transferred to, stored, and processed in countries other than your own, including the United States. These providers maintain their own safeguards for such transfers (e.g., Standard Contractual Clauses), as described in their respective privacy policies linked in Section 7.
